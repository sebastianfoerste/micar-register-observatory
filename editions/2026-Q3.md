# The State of the MiCAR Register — 2026 Q3

MiCAR requires every crypto-asset white paper to be made available in a machine-readable format: Article 6(10) for crypto-assets other than asset-referenced and e-money tokens, Article 19(9) for asset-referenced tokens and Article 51(9) for e-money tokens. Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2984 sets the forms, formats and templates (XHTML with Inline XBRL tagging) and applies from 23 December 2025; this edition does not separate register entries by notification date. Articles 109 and 110 require ESMA to keep the public registers this edition reads, Article 110 being the register of non-compliant entities; the CSV exports used here are ESMA's interim register. Recital 5 of the Implementing Regulation gives the aim of the XBRL mark-up: to facilitate the analysis and comparability of white papers. If disclosures are structured, a regulator can compare them, a competitor can benchmark against them, and a journalist can check them. Structure is what turns a pile of filings into a dataset.

This edition measures a proxy for it: the kind of file each white-paper entry links to, judged by the URL alone.

## What the numbers say

The headline is the share of white papers that link to a URL ending in `.xhtml`/`.html`/`.htm`, `.json` or `.docx`; in this edition all 144 such links are XHTML/HTML and none is JSON or DOCX. It is a proxy, not a count of machine-readable white papers: the URL does not show whether an XHTML file carries Inline XBRL (iXBRL) tagging, a URL with no recognised file extension may lead to one, and this edition did not fetch the files. The XHTML/HTML class can also hold landing pages: 52 of the 144 links end in `/index.html` on a single hosting domain, and the [micar-whitepaper-linter study](https://github.com/sebastianfoerste/micar-whitepaper-linter/tree/main/studies/2026-07-title-ii-annex-i-whitepaper-study) records one of those pages as a landing page that links the XHTML white paper separately. A PDF is readable by a person but not queryable across filings without OCR and parsing; a landing page is not even that — it is a door to a document, not the document. For most white papers on the register, the promise of comparable, checkable disclosure across the single market is a link you still open by hand.

The concentration by Member State is the second story. A handful of home states host most filings, which means a handful of national competent authorities carry most of the supervisory load — and set most of the de facto formatting practice.

## How this was measured

Every figure comes from the [MiCAR Register Observatory](https://github.com/sebastianfoerste/micar-register-observatory), which snapshots the five public ESMA register CSVs weekly and commits the diff. This edition is generated from one dated snapshot, so the numbers are reproducible from that commit. Format is classified by the shape of the linked URL, not by fetching issuer documents; a format is a candidate for structured parsing until the document itself is retrieved.

## What this is not

This edition reports facts about a public register. It makes no assessment of any named issuer, and it does not claim any white paper is unlawful or deficient — a landing-page link is a fact about the register entry, not a judgment about the disclosure behind it. Not legal advice.
