# The State of the MiCAR Register — 2026 Q3

MiCAR requires every crypto-asset white paper to be made available in a machine-readable format: Article 6(10) for crypto-assets other than asset-referenced and e-money tokens, Article 19(9) for asset-referenced tokens and Article 51(9) for e-money tokens. Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2984 sets the forms, formats and templates (XHTML with Inline XBRL tagging). Article 109 requires ESMA to keep the public register this edition reads. The intent is supervision at scale: if disclosures are structured, a regulator can compare them, a competitor can benchmark against them, and a journalist can check them. Structure is what turns a pile of filings into a dataset.

This edition measures a proxy for it: the kind of file each white-paper entry links to, judged by the URL alone.

## What the numbers say

The headline is the share of white-paper links that point to an XHTML/HTML file. It is a proxy, not a count of machine-readable white papers: the URL does not show whether an XHTML file carries Inline XBRL (iXBRL) tagging, a landing page may lead to one, and this edition did not fetch the files. A PDF is readable by a person but not queryable across filings without OCR and parsing; a landing page is not even that — it is a door to a document, not the document. For most white papers on the register, the promise of comparable, checkable disclosure across the single market is a link you still open by hand.

The concentration by Member State is the second story. A handful of home states host most filings, which means a handful of national competent authorities carry most of the supervisory load — and set most of the de facto formatting practice.

## How this was measured

Every figure comes from the [MiCAR Register Observatory](https://github.com/sebastianfoerste/micar-register-observatory), which snapshots the five public ESMA register CSVs weekly and commits the diff. This edition is generated from one dated snapshot, so the numbers are reproducible from that commit. Format is classified by the shape of the linked URL, not by fetching issuer documents; a format is a candidate for structured parsing until the document itself is retrieved.

## What this is not

This edition reports facts about a public register. It makes no assessment of any named issuer, and it does not claim any white paper is unlawful or deficient — a landing-page link is a fact about the register entry, not a judgment about the disclosure behind it. Not legal advice.
