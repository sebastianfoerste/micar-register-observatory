# The State of the MiCAR Register — 2026 Q3

MiCAR's Article 109 requires ESMA to keep crypto-asset white papers in a machine-readable register. The intent is supervision at scale: if disclosures are structured, a regulator can compare them, a competitor can benchmark against them, and a journalist can check them. Structure is what turns a pile of filings into a dataset.

This edition measures how structured the register actually is.

## What the numbers say

The headline is the machine-readable share. A PDF is readable by a person but not queryable across filings without OCR and parsing; a landing page is not even that — it is a door to a document, not the document. For most white papers on the register, the promise of comparable, checkable disclosure across the single market is a link you still open by hand.

The concentration by Member State is the second story. A handful of home states host most filings, which means a handful of national competent authorities carry most of the supervisory load — and set most of the de facto formatting practice.

## How this was measured

Every figure comes from the [MiCAR Register Observatory](https://github.com/sebastianfoerste/micar-register-observatory), which snapshots the five public ESMA register CSVs weekly and commits the diff. This edition is generated from one dated snapshot, so the numbers are reproducible from that commit. Format is classified by the shape of the linked URL, not by fetching issuer documents; a format is a candidate for structured parsing until the document itself is retrieved.

## What this is not

This edition reports facts about a public register. It makes no assessment of any named issuer, and it does not claim any white paper is unlawful or deficient — a landing-page link is a fact about the register entry, not a judgment about the disclosure behind it. Not legal advice.
